# Negative parallelism and reframe constructions (the fatal rule)

This is the single most reliable tell of AI-generated text, and the one rule in this skill with zero tolerance. Peer-reviewed research on LLM stylistic fingerprints backs this up: every major model (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok) produces this pattern dozens of times per response, because it's baked into training data from persuasive writing, TED talks, marketing copy, and op-eds. When a model wants to sound smart, this is its first instinct.

If even one of these appears in a draft, rewrite the entire sentence before moving on. Don't patch around it, delete and restate.

## The banned patterns

- "This isn't X. This is Y."
- "Not X. Y."
- "Forget X. This is Y."
- "Less X, more Y."
- "Not only X, but also Y."
- "It's not just about X, it's about Y."
- "No X, no Y, just Z."
- "X? No. Y."
- "Stop thinking X. Start thinking Y."
- "It's not about X. It's about Y."
- "X is dead. Y is the future."
- "The question isn't X. The question is Y."
- "You don't need X. You need Y."
- "X is overrated. Y is what matters."
- Any sentence that negates one framing then asserts a corrected one.
- Any sentence that rejects an assumption, then replaces it.

## The disguised versions

These wear the same skeleton in a trench coat. Watch for concession-then-pivot structures even when there's no literal "not":

- "While X might seem right, Y is actually..."
- "Sure, X works. But Y is where the real..."
- "X gets all the attention, but Y is what actually..."
- "You might think X. In reality, Y."
- "On the surface, X. Underneath, Y."

## Why it registers as machine-written

The negated framing adds zero information. The reader doesn't need to be told what something ISN'T before learning what it IS. The construction exists purely to manufacture a sense of insight or contrast where none is needed, and readers have absorbed enough AI text by now that their brain flags it instantly: machine.

## The fix

Delete everything before the positive claim. Just state what's true.

- "It's not about the prompt. It's about the context." → "It's about the context."
- "This isn't a bug, it's a feature." → "It's a feature." (or better: say what the feature actually does)
- "We're not just building a tool, we're building a platform." → "We're building a platform." (or, if the distinction genuinely matters, explain the actual difference in plain terms instead of the negation trick)

If the contrast really is the point (two genuinely competing ideas that both matter), state them as two separate factual sentences instead of a negate-then-correct pair. "Most teams optimize for speed. We optimize for accuracy" carries the contrast without the tic, because both halves are affirmative claims, not a rejection followed by a reveal.
