# AI writing patterns to avoid

Beyond banned words and the negative-parallelism tic, research on LLM stylistic fingerprints and Wikipedia's AI-detection field guide document these structural patterns. They make text identifiable as machine-written even when no single banned word appears.

## Puffery and significance inflation

AI inflates the importance of everything: "A pivotal moment in the evolution of..." "Marking a significant shift toward..." "Setting the stage for..." "A key turning point..."

Fix: state the fact. Let the reader judge significance for themselves. Don't tell them how to feel about it.

## Rule of three

AI defaults to listing exactly three things: "speed, efficiency, and innovation." Three adjectives in a row, three short phrases, every time. It's a shortcut to make shallow analysis look comprehensive.

Fix: use 2 things, or 4, or just name the one thing that actually matters. Let the real count of relevant items decide, not a reflex.

## False ranges

"From ancient traditions to modern innovations." Sounds impressive, means nothing. If there's no meaningful middle ground between X and Y, the range is decorative filler.

Fix: delete the range and be specific about the one thing that's actually true.

## Elegant variation

AI's repetition penalty forces it to swap terms to avoid saying the same word twice: a person becomes "the protagonist," then "the key player," then "the eponymous figure," all in the same paragraph.

Fix: just use the name again. Forced synonyms read worse than repetition and actively confuse the reader about whether you mean the same thing.

## Meta-commentary

"In this section, we will discuss..." "Let me walk you through..." "Here's a comprehensive overview of..."

Fix: say the thing. Don't announce that you're about to say the thing.

## Superficial analysis via participle phrases

AI bolts "-ing" phrases onto sentences to fake depth without adding a real claim: "highlighting its importance," "underscoring its significance," "reflecting broader trends," "contributing to the rich tapestry of..."

Fix: delete the participle phrase. If the analysis actually matters, give it its own sentence with a specific, checkable claim.

## Knowledge-cutoff disclaimers

"As of my last update..." "While specific details are limited..." "Based on available information..."

Fix: never include these in final prose. If information is genuinely uncertain, hedge plainly ("I think," "probably") instead of gesturing at a training cutoff.

## Collaborative communication leakage

"I hope this helps!" "Would you like me to..." "Certainly!" "Of course!" "Great question!"

These belong in a chat reply, not in a document, email, or report. Strip them from anything meant to be published or sent as a finished piece.

## Metronome rhythm

Every sentence roughly the same length. Every paragraph the same number of sentences. Perfectly even pacing start to finish. It reads smooth but has no texture, which is itself a tell.

Fix: real writing breathes unevenly. Short. Then longer. Then a fragment. Then a sentence that runs long because it earns the length.

## Copulative avoidance

AI replaces plain "is" and "has" with inflated alternatives: "serves as," "stands as," "represents," "marks a," "holds the distinction of being."

Fix: just say "is." Simple verbs work and don't call attention to themselves.

## Title case in headers

AI capitalizes every main word in headers: "Global Context: Critical Mineral Demand." Humans overwhelmingly default to sentence case.

Fix: sentence case headers. Capitalize only the first word and proper nouns.
